01 · Core Concepts

From Bits to Qubits: Why Quantum Is Different

Classical computers store information in binary switches that are strictly 0 or 1. A qubit lives in a continuous two-dimensional complex vector space, unlocking superposition and phase interference.

Classical Bit

Rigid Binary: {0, 1}
  • Discrete two-point state: At any instant, a bit holds strictly 0 or 1 with zero intermediate superposition.
  • No relative phase: Even a classical coin flip only carries real positive probabilities, never negative or imaginary amplitudes.
  • Only one non-trivial 1-bit gate: The classical NOT gate flips 0 ↔ 1, whereas a qubit admits infinite continuous rotations.

Quantum Qubit

|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩
  • Complex superposition: Exists as a normalized vector |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ where both α, β ∈ ℂ.
  • Four cardinal phase axes: States |+⟩, |−⟩, |+i⟩, |−i⟩ all measure 50% / 50%, yet differ physically by phase +1, −1, +i, −i.
  • Reversible interference: Unitary gates rotate the state vector smoothly so amplitudes can reinforce or cancel cleanly.
Did You Know? Why H → H Is NOT a Random Coin Flip

Applying one Hadamard gate to |0⟩ creates an equal 50/50 superposition |+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2. If this were a classical coin flip, applying a second randomizer would stay 50/50. Instead, applying a second H causes the |1⟩ amplitudes (+1/2 and −1/2) to destructively cancel while |0⟩ constructively interferes back to 100% |0⟩!

02 · Fundamental Theory

The 7 Physics Laws Behind This Lab

Every button, complex matrix step, 2D phase arrow, and 3D Bloch coordinate in the workbench below is governed directly by these seven postulates of single-qubit quantum mechanics.

Law 01

Hilbert Space Postulate

|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩,   α, β ∈ ℂ

Every pure single-qubit state is represented by a unit column vector in a two-dimensional complex Hilbert space ℂ² spanned by orthonormal basis kets |0⟩ and |1⟩.

Law 02

Principle of Superposition

H|0⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩) / √2

Any normalized complex linear combination of valid basis states is itself a valid physical quantum state, placing the qubit simultaneously across |0⟩ and |1⟩.

Law 03

Unitary Evolution & Conservation

U†U = I,   |α|² + |β|² = 1

Closed quantum gates act as reversible 2×2 unitary matrices that preserve vector length so total probability always remains exactly 1.0000.

Law 04

Born’s Rule

P(0) = |α|²,   P(1) = |β|²

Measurement probabilities equal the squared modulus of each complex amplitude: |z|² = Re(z)² + Im(z)², bridging complex wave mechanics to observable statistics.

Law 05

Wavefunction Collapse

|ψ⟩ → |0⟩ or |1⟩ (Projective)

Measuring a qubit in the computational basis irreversibly projects the superposition onto either |0⟩ or |1⟩; repeated 100-shot ensembles reveal Born’s Rule statistically.

Law 06

Interference & Relative Phase

Δφ = arg(β) − arg(α)

Global phase is unobservable, but the relative phase Δφ between α and β distinguishes |+⟩, |−⟩, |+i⟩, and |−i⟩ and drives constructive or destructive interference.

Law 07 · Geometric Unification

Bloch Sphere Geometry

Any pure single-qubit state maps uniquely onto a point (x, y, z) on the surface of a unit 3D sphere via the Pauli expectation values ⟨ψ|σx,y,z|ψ⟩. Polar angle θ encodes measurement probabilities while azimuthal angle φ encodes relative complex phase Δφ.

x = 2 · Re(α*β) = sin(θ) cos(φ) y = 2 · Im(α*β) = sin(θ) sin(φ) z = |α|² − |β|² = cos(θ)
03 · Unitary Operators

Meet the 6 Quantum Gates

Each gate in the simulator is a 2×2 complex unitary matrix that rotates the qubit state vector cleanly around an axis of the Bloch sphere.

X

Pauli-X

180° rotation about X-axis
0110

Quantum NOT (bit-flip) gate that swaps |0⟩ and |1⟩ amplitudes without altering phase alignment.

Y

Pauli-Y

180° rotation about Y-axis
0−ii0

Simultaneous bit-flip and imaginary phase rotation: maps |0⟩ → i|1⟩ and |1⟩ → −i|0⟩.

Z

Pauli-Z

180° rotation about Z-axis
100−1

Phase-flip gate leaving |0⟩ intact while negating |1⟩ (Δφ shifts by 180°: |+⟩ ↔ |−⟩).

H

Hadamard

180° about (X+Z)/√2
1/√2
111−1

Superposition engine transforming basis states into equal superpositions (|0⟩ → |+⟩, |1⟩ → |−⟩) and interfering them back.

S

Phase Gate (S)

+90° (π/2) about Z-axis
100i

Quarter-turn complex phase gate multiplying |1⟩ by +i, rotating |+⟩ → |+i⟩ (and S² = Z).

I

Identity

0° (Null evolution)
1001

Leaves every amplitude and complex phase untouched; represents an idle quantum wire interval.

03B · Real-World Impact

Where This Matters in the Real World

Quantum Algorithms (Grover & Shor)

Hadamard superposition paired with phase flips (Z, S) enables amplitude amplification in Grover’s unstructured search and quantum Fourier period-finding in Shor’s factoring algorithm.

Quantum Cryptography (BB84 QKD)

Encodes secret key bits across conjugate bases (|0⟩, |1⟩ and |+⟩, |−⟩). Any eavesdropper who measures in the wrong basis collapses the state and introduces detectable errors.

Interactive STEM Physics Education

Bridges abstract linear algebra and Hilbert-space notation with live geometric intuition so physics and engineering students can verify unitary transformations hands-on.
